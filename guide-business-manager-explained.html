Meta Business Manager Explained: BMs, Ad Accounts, Limits & Roles

“BM3”, “BM10”, “verified BM” — if the terminology is fuzzy, this guide makes it clear. Here’s how Meta Business Manager actually works.

ScaleShield · Operator guide · 6 min read

What is a Business Manager?

A Business Manager (BM) is the top-level container that holds your advertising assets — ad accounts, Pages, pixels, and the people who access them. Think of it as the structure everything else lives inside.

Business Managers vs ad accounts

An ad account is where campaigns run and budgets are spent. A Business Manager holds one or more ad accounts. That’s why package names map to account counts: a BM3 is a Business Manager containing 3 ad accounts, a BM10 holds 10, and so on.

Ad-account limits (and how they grow)

New ad accounts start with conservative daily spend limits. Those ceilings rise as the account spends cleanly and builds trust — which is why warm-up matters so much. Don’t judge an account by its starting limit.

Roles & access

Access is granted per person and per asset — admin vs employee, and account-level vs BM-level. The principle that keeps things clean: give the minimum access needed, and operate from your own profile rather than the source you were handed.

Verified Business Managers

A verified BM has completed Meta’s business verification, which generally means a stronger trust profile, better deliverability, and higher ceilings. It’s why a verified BM3 can outperform a larger unverified setup.

Sharing assets safely

When you receive a ready profile, treat it as a distributor: share the assets out to your own working profile and operate from there. And always keep a backup profile with the same assets shared to it, so a single profile going down never costs you access. See the full policy & setup playbook.
